## Background

The tritium fuel cycle is the critical unsolved supply chain problem for commercial deuterium-tritium fusion. Every fusion company building a D-T plant needs the same instrumentation layer — valves, pressure gauges, temperature sensors, composition analyzers, flow controllers, safety monitors — and none of the standard industrial versions work in a tritium environment.

Beta decay from tritium destroys organic materials (seals, plastics, lubricants) within weeks. Tritium permeates through metals at elevated temperatures. Electronics degrade in radiation fields. The result: **the entire COTS instrument supply chain simply does not exist for this environment.**

These documents map that gap, survey what currently exists (and what doesn’t), and propose a portfolio of 12 products that together address the full instrumentation needs of a tritium fuel cycle from plasma exhaust to breeding blanket extraction.

## Key Findings

### On measurement technologies

- No commercial real-time tritium flow meter exists
- All current inventory methods are batch processes requiring hours to days
- The field uses a patchwork of 6+ indirect techniques, none designed for continuous process control
- Speed-of-sound composition measurement (2023) and graphene proton sieving are the most promising emerging approaches

### On the patent landscape

- Zero patents exist for tritium-specific Coriolis flow measurement
- Raman spectroscopy for tritium composition is being commercialised by IS-Instruments (UKAEA contract, 2024) but not yet patented
- Graphene isotope separation (separation factor ~30 for H vs T vs ~1.8 for cryogenic distillation) is published science with no instrumentation patents filed
- Five high-value patent opportunities identified — strongest is a combined isotope-compensated flow + composition sensor

### On the product portfolio

- **$380M combined TAM** across 12 products
- **4 products are launch-ready** (Tier 1) — addressable in existing nuclear tritium market before fusion plants are built
- **UNITY-2** (Kyoto Fusioneering + CNL, commissioning late 2026) is the first fusion validation customer
- The **digital twin / accountancy platform** (ISO-DA09) is the long-term moat — every hardware sensor becomes a data node, creating SaaS recurring revenue with regulatory lock-in

## The Opportunity in One Paragraph

Kyoto Fusioneering, Commonwealth Fusion Systems, STEP, TAE, Helion and 40+ other fusion companies are all building tritium fuel cycles. Each one needs dozens of instruments that don’t exist yet. The first company to build a complete, fusion-qualified instrumentation suite — and integrate it into a regulatory-compliant accountancy platform — will become the Endress+Hauser of fusion energy. Unlike the reactor itself, instrumentation is a near-term, engineering-solvable, capital-efficient problem with revenue available from existing nuclear tritium facilities *today*, before commercial fusion is proven.
