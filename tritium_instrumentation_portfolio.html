Strategic Rationale

The core insight: Every fusion company building a D-T fuel cycle faces the same instrumentation and control problem. The components needed — valves, sensors, gauges, monitors — are standard industrial products. But none of the standard versions work with tritium. Beta decay destroys organics. Tritium permeates through plastics and most metals. Electronics fail in radiation fields. The entire COTS instrument supply chain simply doesn't exist for this environment.

ISOTRACE builds the tritium-compatible instrumentation layer that sits between the process components (pumps, separation columns, storage beds) and the plant control system. We are not competing with Kyoto Fusioneering — we are supplying them. Every fuel cycle system they build needs dozens of our instruments.

Business model: Component sales (hardware), software licenses (digital twin / accountancy platform), and long-term service/recalibration contracts. First revenue from existing nuclear tritium facilities (CANDU operators, Savannah River), then fusion test facilities (UNITY-2), then first-of-a-kind fusion pilot plants from 2030 onward.

ISO-AM04 · Atmosphere Monitor
Room-Level Tritium-in-Air Monitor
Continuous ionization chamber monitor, 1 MBq/m³ to 100 GBq/m³
Worker safety requires continuous real-time tritium monitoring in gloveboxes, laboratories, and facility air handling systems. Existing monitors (Berthold, Eckert & Ziegler) suffer from the "tritium memory effect" — once exposed to tritium, the ionization chamber walls become contaminated and give false high readings for months. ISO-AM04 uses a replaceable PTFE-lined inner cartridge with 90-day service intervals, combined with a novel cross-calibration algorithm using a small sealed reference source to correct for wall contamination in real time. ISO-FM05 · Flow
Differential Pressure Mass Flow Controller
Tritium-rated, isotope-compensated mass flow for fuel injection
A simpler-than-Coriolis approach: two radiation-hard pressure transducers straddling a precision orifice plate. Mass flow is computed from the pressure drop using a compensation model that corrects for the H/D/T isotope ratio (measured by inline Raman or input from the upstream ISS). No moving parts. No vibrating tubes. No maglev bearings. Accuracy of ±0.5% is achievable with isotope correction — sufficient for plasma fueling control. The orifice plate is replaceable in 15 minutes through a glovebox-compatible interface, solving the lifetime problem.

ISO-RS06 · Spectroscopy
Inline Raman Isotope Analyzer
Real-time H/D/T composition in process gas streams
Raman spectroscopy is the only technique that can distinguish all six hydrogen isotopologues (H₂, HD, HT, D₂, DT, T₂) simultaneously and non-destructively. UKAEA's IS-Instruments GRADE project is advancing this but remains research-stage. ISO-RS06 is the engineered product version — a fiber-coupled hollow-core fiber Raman probe that mounts through a standard CF40 viewport, with radiation-hardened near-infrared fiber (proven at JET), remote spectrometer electronics, and factory calibration against all six isotopologues. The proprietary calibration software handles the non-linear response curves identified in TLK Karlsruhe's FTIR work.

ISO-CT08 · Calorimetry
Self-Assaying Tritium Storage Bed Calorimeter
Real-time inventory measurement for ZrCo/uranium getter storage systems
ITER's current SDS design measures tritium inventory by helium-loop calorimetry: measure how much heat the decaying tritium generates, infer the mass. The problem is it takes 24+ hours to reach steady state. ISO-CT08 reduces this to under 2 hours using a multi-point thermal sensor array embedded directly in the bed matrix, combined with a dynamic thermal model (vs. steady-state only) that can extrapolate to final reading early. The "self-assaying" concept — where the bed itself is the primary measurement device without needing to transfer tritium — is the key innovation. ±0.5g accuracy at 100g inventory in 2 hours.

ISO-RC13 · Remote Control
Radiation-Hard Process Control Interface
DCS/PLC integration module for tritium facility automation
All tritium instrumentation generates signals that need to reach a plant control system. The problem: signal cables running from inside a tritium glove box to an external DCS must themselves be radiation-compatible, hermetically sealed at every penetration, and electrically isolated to prevent tritium from migrating along conductor surfaces. ISO-RC13 is a standardized I/O module designed specifically for tritium plant penetration panels — fiber optic signal transmission (eliminates migration path), hermetic glass-ceramic connectors, and a standardized software driver package for Siemens PCS7, DeltaV, and Ignition SCADA systems.

Business Model

⚙️
Hardware Sales
Component sales to fuel cycle system integrators (Kyoto Fusioneering, CNL, ITER) and directly to fusion companies. ASPs range from $8k (thermocouples) to $180k (Raman analyzer). Typical fuel cycle requires 40–120 instrumentation points.
🔄
Service & Recalibration
Tritium instruments require periodic recalibration against traceable standards — and those standards must themselves be maintained in tritium environments. Annual recalibration contracts at 15–25% of hardware value create predictable recurring revenue with high switching costs.
💻
Software Platform (ISO-DA09)
SaaS licensing of the digital twin / accountancy platform. Priced per facility ($200k–$800k/year) based on tritium inventory scale. Every hardware product becomes a node in this network. Regulatory pressure (IAEA safeguards, NRC licensing) makes this non-optional for commercial fusion plants.
🏛️
Government R&D Contracts
DOE INFUSE program, UKAEA Fusion Industry Programme, EU Fusion for Energy grants. Each Tier 3 product has a natural government R&D funding path. This non-dilutive capital funds the high-risk products while Tier 1/2 hardware generates commercial revenue.
🧪
Test & Qualification Services
Third-party component qualification using the tritium test protocols developed for ISO products. Pump manufacturers, valve suppliers, and seal vendors all need tritium exposure testing. ISOTRACE's test infrastructure (built for product development) becomes a revenue-generating service for the broader supply chain.
🌐
IP Licensing
The graphene isotope sensor (ISO-GS11) and permeation sensor (ISO-PS10) represent genuinely novel physics that larger industrial instrument companies (Emerson, ABB, Yokogawa) cannot quickly replicate. Long-term licensing deals provide upside beyond ISOTRACE's direct sales capacity.

Key Risks

Fusion timeline slippage
Commercial fusion plants delayed beyond 2035. Mitigation: Tier 1 products generate revenue from existing fission/nuclear tritium market regardless of fusion timeline.
MEDIUM
Tritium access for testing
Validating products requires tritium exposure — access gated by national labs. Mitigation: Establish partnership with CNL/SRNL early; design deuterium-surrogate testing protocols for pre-tritium validation stages.
HIGH
Nuclear regulatory burden
NRC/nuclear authority qualification adds 2–4 years to product timelines. Mitigation: Design products to existing qualified standards (ASME, ISA-S67) from day one; hire regulatory affairs expertise early.
MEDIUM
Large incumbent entry
Emerson, ABB, or Yokogawa decides to build tritium instrumentation. Mitigation: These companies won't move until market is proven; ISOTRACE will be 5+ years ahead with IP, customer relationships, and regulatory approvals.
LOW
ISO-GS11 graphene physics
Graphene isotope separation may not work at process conditions. Mitigation: This is one of 12 products; portfolio is not dependent on it. Fund as a long-term R&D bet, not a core product.
HIGH (contained)
Dual-use / export controls
Tritium instrumentation may be subject to nuclear export controls, limiting international sales. Mitigation: Structure IP ownership and manufacturing in a single jurisdiction (UK or Canada recommended); engage export control counsel early.
